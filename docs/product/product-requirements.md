# Product Requirements Brief

## Problem
Fuel-station workflows combine physical measurement, machine readings, shifts, deliveries, payments, credit and supplier obligations. Records may be fragmented. Operators need timely reconciliation and explainable exceptions.

## Users
Owner (policy, approvals, reporting); manager (shift close and exceptions); attendant (assigned readings and handover); accountant (financial review); technician (maintenance); auditor (read-only traceability).

## MVP stories
- Attendant submits opening/closing meter and dip readings for assigned shift.
- Manager sees shift completeness and missing records.
- Owner inspects expected versus measured stock with formula and evidence.
- Accountant compares validated sales with payment allocations.
- Manager reviews delivery evidence and opens a dispute.
- Owner asks questions and receives answers linked to authorised records.
- Approver accepts/rejects a specific proposal with audit record.

## Principles
Accuracy before automation; explain every discrepancy; human authority for consequential actions; least privilege; synthetic data in public demo; autonomy only after evaluation.

## Future success measures
Shift completeness; time from close to reconciliation; evidence completeness; duplicate-event rate; anomaly false-positive/negative rates; human override/rejection; user trust. No impact claim until measured in a controlled pilot.
